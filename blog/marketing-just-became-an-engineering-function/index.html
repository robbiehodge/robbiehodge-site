Marketing Just Became
an Engineering Function.

How I used Codex to turn a gap in our marketing funnel into a live visitor follow-up system in a single day.

Author
Robbie Hodge
Published
August 19, 2026
Read time
6 minutes
Category
AI · Marketing Ops · Automation

I had an idea in the morning.

By the afternoon, it was running.

The idea started with a gap in our funnel.

We could identify some of the people visiting our website, but if they left without completing a form or making an inquiry, that interest disappeared. The data existed, but nothing meaningful happened with it.

I wanted to change that.

The goal was to build a system that could identify a visitor, enrich the contact, send the data into our CRM, wait 24 hours, check whether the person converted, and send personalized follow-up if they did not.

That sounds like a development project.

It used to be one.

From an Idea to Working Infrastructure

I used Codex to help me investigate the existing marketing stack and understand how the data was moving.

The first step was not building anything. It was determining how the visitor identification platform was connected to the CRM.

We traced the integration, inspected the available fields, and confirmed that visitor data could be passed into HubSpot using two dedicated contact properties: Apollo Website Visitor and Apollo Last Website Visit.

From there, we built the complete flow.

How the Automation Works

When the visitor identification platform recognizes a new person on the website, the system now:

  1. 01Identifies the visitor.
  2. 02Enriches the person with available contact information.
  3. 03Marks the record as a website visitor.
  4. 04Pushes the contact into HubSpot.
  5. 05Sets the person as a marketing contact.
  6. 06Assigns the appropriate contact owner.
  7. 07Waits 24 hours.
  8. 08Checks whether the person converted.
  9. 09Sends a personalized email if they did not convert.

The final result is simple from the recipient's perspective. They visit a website. If they leave without taking action, they receive a relevant, personal follow-up the next day.

Behind that email is a connected system moving data between platforms, evaluating conversion activity, protecting existing records, and deciding whether the message should be sent.

The Safeguards Mattered as Much as the Automation

Getting records from one platform into another was only part of the project.

The system also needed safeguards.

We configured it so contacts cannot enter the workflow twice. Existing contact owners are protected. Ownership is only updated when the owner is blank or matches the designated fallback owner.

Only newly qualified contacts enter after activation. Existing database records are not unexpectedly pulled into the campaign.

Contacts who convert during the 24-hour waiting period skip the email entirely.

Every incoming contact is marked correctly for marketing eligibility, and the email is sent from the intended person with the correct reply address and subscription settings.

Those details are what separate a working demo from infrastructure that can safely run every day.

Starting With a Controlled Launch

I did not begin by sending thousands of records through it.

The visitor platform had identified more than 2,000 people during the previous seven days. Pulling all of them would have consumed most of the available enrichment credits and created an unnecessarily large first send.

Instead, I selected a controlled pilot of 25 net-new, high-match visitors.

The contacts entered the enrichment workflow, moved into HubSpot, qualified for enrollment, and paused at the 24-hour delay exactly as intended.

That confirmed the complete connection was working. The system was live.

What Codex Changed

AI is usually discussed as a faster way to write copy, summarize documents, or generate ideas. That is useful, but it undersells what is happening.

Codex helped me inspect two live platforms, understand how data was mapped, configure the automation, build the email, add safeguards, test a real contact, diagnose integration issues, launch a controlled pilot, and verify the results.

I still made the decisions.

I defined the opportunity, evaluated the risks, chose the audience, approved the messaging, and determined when the system was ready to go live.

“Codex compressed the distance between the decision and the execution.”

The New Marketing Skill Set

This is where marketing is heading.

The most valuable marketers will not only create campaigns. They will connect systems, design workflows, shape customer experiences, and build the infrastructure that makes growth repeatable.

They will be able to see a gap in the funnel in the morning and deploy a working solution that afternoon.

No development ticket. No agency handoff. No multiweek sprint.

Just an idea, clear judgment, the right tools, and the ability to execute.

The automation now runs continuously. New visitors can enter the system, move through the CRM, wait for the appropriate moment, and receive personalized follow-up without anyone manually exporting a list or sending an email.

Idea in the morning.
In inboxes by afternoon.
Running forever.

Marketing just became an engineering function.
