Fractional CMO

What is a fractional CMO
and how does it work?

A fractional CMO is a senior marketing executive who works with a business on a part-time or defined-schedule basis. They provide the same strategic capability as a full-time CMO — strategy, team leadership, agency management, budget ownership, and revenue accountability — without the $200,000–$350,000 annual salary, benefits, equity, or 3–6 month recruiting timeline of a full-time hire. The term "fractional" refers to the time commitment, not the quality of the work.

A marketing consultant typically provides advice, recommendations, or deliverables on specific questions or projects. A fractional CMO owns the marketing function — they are embedded in your team, accountable to revenue outcomes, and responsible for execution, not just advice. The distinction is accountability and ownership. A consultant tells you what to do. A fractional CMO does it with you and is measured on the results.

A marketing agency executes specific deliverables — ads, content, SEO, social — and is typically accountable to activity metrics like impressions and clicks. A fractional CMO is a senior executive who owns the strategy and is accountable to business outcomes like revenue and pipeline. A fractional CMO can manage your agencies on your behalf, write briefs, hold them accountable, and make sure every agency is working toward the same goal. Most businesses need both — an agency for execution and a CMO to lead it.

Day-to-day activities vary by engagement but typically include leading weekly marketing meetings, reviewing and approving campaigns, managing agency and vendor relationships, mentoring in-house marketing staff, making strategic decisions on channel mix and budget allocation, and reporting on the metrics that connect to revenue. On a strategic level, a fractional CMO is constantly looking at the market, the competition, and the data to make sure the company is making the right moves.

You probably need a fractional CMO if: your marketing is not generating predictable revenue; you have a marketing team but no senior leader setting strategy; you are spending money on agencies with no clear accountability; you are scaling and need marketing to keep up; or you are post-acquisition and need marketing leadership embedded quickly. If any of those sound familiar, a 30-minute call will tell you whether a fractional engagement makes sense for your specific situation.

Digital Marketing

Channels, strategy,
and what actually works.

The highest-performing eCommerce brands build a multi-channel flywheel: content and YouTube for top-of-funnel awareness and SEO, email and lifecycle marketing for retention and repeat revenue, and paid media for acquisition that scales. The specific mix depends on the brand, the product, and the margin structure. What consistently underperforms is over-reliance on any single channel — especially paid acquisition without a retention layer to justify the customer acquisition cost.

YouTube is the highest-ROI content channel available to eCommerce brands that can execute it well. A well-built YouTube channel creates compounding organic traffic, builds deep brand trust, and drives purchase intent at scale. Robbie scaled a specialty DTC brand to 1.6 million YouTube subscribers and #1 category ranking through content-led growth — it is one of his core areas of expertise. The brands that invested in YouTube five years ago are now the hardest to compete with.

The most effective B2B lead generation in 2026 combines outbound with intent signals and warm follow-up. Tools like Apollo identify who is visiting your site and match them to contact data — which turns anonymous traffic into a warm outbound list. HubSpot sequences then deliver personalized follow-up automatically. Layered with strong content, clear positioning, and a tight ICP, this approach consistently outperforms cold outbound alone. The secret is not the tools — it is the quality of the targeting and the messaging.

AI search engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overview pull from structured content, authoritative sources, and factual pages with clear schema markup. Brands that want to appear in AI search results need to invest in structured FAQ pages, key facts tables, comprehensive About pages, and content that directly answers the questions their prospects are asking. This is a shift from keyword density toward information density — the brands that win in AI search are the ones that are the clearest and most comprehensive sources of truth about what they do.
